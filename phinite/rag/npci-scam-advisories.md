# UPI Safety Advisories — common payment scams and what to do
Prepared for the Paytm Agent Pay Risk Assessor. Written in the spirit of NPCI / RBI public advisories. Attach this file (or a collection containing it) to the Risk Assessor and Scam Guardian nodes.

## The one rule that defeats most UPI scams
You never have to pay, enter your UPI PIN, or approve a "collect request" in order to RECEIVE money. Entering your PIN always SENDS money. Anyone who says otherwise is trying to take money from you.

## 1. Utility disconnection threat ("your electricity will be cut tonight")
**How it looks:** SMS or WhatsApp claiming your electricity / gas / water will be disconnected tonight because a bill or "KYC" is pending. Asks you to call a number or pay a small amount (often ₹1–₹10) to a personal UPI ID such as `bescom-update@ybl`.
**Red flags:** urgency ("tonight"), a personal UPI handle instead of a registered biller, a mobile number instead of the official helpline, spelling errors, a request to install an app.
**What to do:** Do not pay. Check and pay bills only inside the Paytm Electricity / Bills section, where billers are verified. Report the number and UPI ID.

## 2. Small payment to "update KYC" or "unlock a refund"
**How it looks:** "Your KYC has expired, pay ₹1 to re-verify" or "Your refund of ₹2,499 is ready — pay ₹1 / accept this request to receive it." The sender uses a handle like `kyc-verify@okaxis` or `refund-helpdesk@ybl`.
**Why it works:** the ₹1 "payment" is actually a collect request or a screen-share session that lets the scammer drain the account.
**What to do:** KYC is never done over WhatsApp. Refunds never require a payment. Decline any collect request you did not initiate. Report the handle.

## 3. Lottery, lucky draw, prize
**How it looks:** "Congratulations! You have won ₹5,00,000 in the Paytm Lucky Draw. Pay ₹999 processing fee to `lucky-draw@ibl` to release the prize."
**Red flags:** you never entered any draw; a fee is required before the prize; pressure to act within hours.
**What to do:** Do not pay. There is no Paytm lucky draw that charges a fee. Report and block.

## 4. OTP / UPI PIN sharing
**How it looks:** A caller pretending to be from your bank, Paytm, a courier or the police asks you to read out an OTP or enter your UPI PIN "to verify" or "to receive a delivery refund".
**What to do:** Never share an OTP or PIN with anyone, for any reason. Hang up. Genuine staff will never ask for it.

## 5. Fake customer care and remote-access apps
**How it looks:** A "support executive" from a number found on Google or social media asks you to install AnyDesk / TeamViewer / a screen-share app to "fix" a failed payment.
**What to do:** Use only the in-app Help section. Never install remote-access apps at a stranger's request. Report the number.

## 6. Job, task or registration fee
**How it looks:** "Part-time work from home — earn ₹3,000/day liking videos. Pay ₹500 registration fee." Small early payouts build trust, then large "deposits" are demanded.
**What to do:** Genuine employers never charge fees. Do not pay. Report the Telegram/WhatsApp group.

## How Paytm Agent Pay scores a payee
- **Low (0–34):** verified Paytm merchant with a long, clean transaction history. Safe to proceed after the user approves.
- **Medium (35–69):** unknown handle with little or no history. Proceed only if the user personally knows the payee; say so plainly.
- **High (70–100):** multiple fraud complaints, recently created handle, or impersonation of a bank/utility/government body. Do not pay. Explain, offer to report.

## If money was already sent
Call 1930 (national cyber-crime helpline) immediately and report at cybercrime.gov.in. Report in-app so the payee can be flagged. The faster the report, the higher the chance of a freeze.
